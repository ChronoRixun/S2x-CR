Five takes on the same brief

Two pages already existed. Three of us then worked the same brief independently — a written spec with the audience, the factual copy, the 103 in-game captures and one hard honesty constraint. This page lines them all up so you can see what each one reached for, and what the final build takes from each.

Previews are live pages rendered at 1280px and scaled down. Click through to open any of them properly.

The brief, in one paragraph

S2x-CR is a community client that brings Call of Duty: WWII back — dedicated servers, bot-filled lobbies, the Headquarters progression economy rebuilt. The reader is a lapsed player who found the link in a Discord, owns the game on Steam, and assumes it's dead. The goal is not to impress them. It is to get them to download the build and join a server tonight. One constraint bound every entry: the server browser captures all read 0/18 players, and no design was allowed to imply otherwise.

PRIOR · marketing landing page

The front door

Prior attempt · built by GPT

What it got right

Someone landing cold knows what this is in five seconds and where to click. Restrained gold-on-charcoal, good typographic rhythm, real alt text, a skip link, visible focus rings. Mobile holds up. It is the handsomest of the five.

Where it falls down

The evidence is thin — six screenshots against the other page's twenty-five, and the specifics that make this project believable got flattened into taglines. It asserts rather than shows. No Open Graph tags at all, which matters when the link is pasted into Discord constantly. Full-resolution JPEGs used as grid thumbnails: 11 MB over a scroll.

Taken forward: the typographic discipline, the CTA hierarchy, and the fact that a stranger should never have to work out what they're looking at.

The dossier

Prior attempt · built by Claude · currently live at chronorixun.github.io/S2x

25 screenshotsclaim → proofno CTA

What it got right

It is convincing. Every claim is followed immediately by a capture proving it, and the captions are specific and unhedged — “4v3 with bots, full scoreboard and progression”, “Five level-ups in a single match, five drops credited”. That copy is the best asset this project has and it became the factual source for every other entry here.

Where it falls down

Two hard faults. It has no doctype, so browsers render it in quirks mode. It has no viewport meta, so a phone lays it out at 980px and scales down until the body text is unreadable — and most arrivals are on a phone, from Discord. Beyond that: no description meta, no OG tags, and no call to action anywhere until the very bottom. It documents; it never asks for anything.

Taken forward: the evidence density and the hard-number captions. Those are the credibility engine and the final build keeps them.

The three candidates

Same brief, same screenshots, no contact between the three. The striking result is that two of the three independently arrived at the same thesis — that the honest 0/18 server browser should be the centrepiece rather than something to bury.

CANDIDATE · Kimi K3

“The empty slots are the invitation”

Candidate · Kimi K3 via Hermes

best line on the page21 screenshots10 sections

Its concept, in its own words

“Make the empty server browser the pitch instead of hiding it… the infrastructure is real, four different hosts already run it, and the empty slots are the reader’s invitation, with bot fill guaranteeing a match the second they join.”

What it saw that the others didn’t

  • The stat row. Nine servers · four hosts · 19–154 ms ping · “0/18 players — the part that’s on you.” That last line does more work than any paragraph any of us wrote. It takes the single weakest fact and turns it into a direct address.
  • “The match pays out” as its own beat, placed immediately after the bots section. For a lapsed player the emotional hook isn’t that bots exist — it’s that progress counts again.
  • Discord placed next to the one screenshot with real humans in it. Ask people to come and meet people at the exact moment you show them people.
Taken forward: the stat row almost verbatim, the payout section, and the Discord placement.

CANDIDATE · MiniMax

“The servers are empty. That’s the whole problem S2x solves.”

Candidate · MiniMax M3 via Hermes · copy by MiniMax, markup assembled here

best headline producedsharpest critique8 screenshots

Its concept, in its own words

“A retired player’s phone is full of places that ask them to believe. The most credible thing on that screen is the server browser they can’t fake… I’ll treat the empty player counts as the headline, not the footnote.”

What it saw that the others didn’t

  • That headline. Nine words that name the weakness and resolve it in the same breath. Neither of the other two wrote anything close, and it is now the h1 on the final build.
  • The risk nobody else spotted. All three of us independently decided to lead with the honest 0/18. MiniMax was the only one to then attack its own idea: “a skeptical reader could read 0/18 as ‘dead project’ in the first second and bounce before the supporting evidence lands… if that bridge is even one beat slow, the honesty reads as weakness.”
  • The download block placed early, and the Steam requirement stated plainly up front rather than buried — “that’s the trust collapse point if left unsaid”.
Taken forward: the headline verbatim, the early download block, and the Steam line. Its own risk warning is why the final build answers the objection in the headline itself rather than waiting for the reader to scroll.

MiniMax runs on a remote gateway and its replies were truncated above roughly 500 words, so it produced concept, structure, critique and finished copy, and the HTML was assembled here from that direction. The words on its page are its own.

CANDIDATE · Claude

“Isn’t it dead?”

Candidate · Claude (Opus 5)

server list as real text18 screenshotscontainer queries

Its concept

Structure the whole page as an honest answer to the one question a skeptic actually has, and put that question in the navigation. Nothing gets sold before the objection is met.

What it saw that the others didn’t

  • The server browser transcribed into a real HTML table, sitting beside the screenshot. The most important evidence on the site was trapped in a JPEG that is illegible on a phone — which is where most readers are. As markup it is readable, selectable, searchable and indexable, and it keeps the colour-coded host names that prove the launcher's colour codes work end to end.
  • Column dropping keyed to the panel, not the viewport, via container queries — the table sits in a half-width column on desktop and full width on mobile, so viewport breakpoints get it wrong in the middle.
  • Captions carrying the raw numbers — “58 kills · 39 headshots · 7,550”, “Credits available in 3h 58m 41s” — inherited from the dossier and pushed further.
Taken forward: the transcribed server table, the container-query handling, and the objection-first running order.
