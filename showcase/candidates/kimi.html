S2x-CR v1.3.0 · community client for Call of Duty: WWII

WWII’s multiplayer isn’t dead. It needed new servers.

Retail matchmaking is empty and the progression servers are gone. S2x-CR brings the game back: dedicated servers you can run yourself, bots that fill every lobby, and the Headquarters economy — ranks, supply drops, orders — working again.

02

An empty server is never an empty match

Set bot_fill 17 and every match fills itself on map start — TDM, Domination, Free-for-All, Hardpoint, and even War mode with full 9v9 teams. Join a 0/18 server and you’re in a real game seconds later, not a lobby waiting room.

Three name pools, 54 names each, shuffled every map — a different lobby every game. And in offline Custom Matches, where bots used to silently never spawn, the fork routes bot_fill and spawnBot through the game’s own spawn script on listen servers: bots pick teams and classes and actually walk onto the map, capped at the free slots (17 bots plus the host).

03

The match pays out

Retail WWII’s end-of-match screen always promised a Rare Supply Drop on every soldier level-up — and the dead servers never delivered it. The fork’s economy finally credits it: every level-up pays out, on dedicated servers and in Local Play alike.

04

The Headquarters economy, running locally

Orders, contracts, payroll, supply drops, the Quartermaster and Mail — all re-implemented locally over the Achievement Engine protocol. Earn rewards, complete challenges and open drops exactly the way the retail game did it, because the retail UI is what’s drawing all of this.

05

Supply drops open like they used to

The full card-flip animation, Common through Legendary — calling cards, weapon charms, emotes, pistol grips, uniforms — with duplicates converting to Armory Credits. Or skip the menu: walk to the marker in Headquarters, watch the crate come down, and open it on foot.

06

Headquarters with actual people in it

The social space works: real players standing around with their ranks overhead, the 1v1 pit waiting for challengers. This is the part bots can’t fake — and the part that gets better with every person who installs.

07

Zombies got the economy too

Groesten Haus and The Final Reich are playable with full progression — wall buys, the Mystery Box, consumables, wave tracking — and the HQ economy extends into Nazi Zombies with its own orders, contracts and skull-backed supply drops. Captures also show The Shadowed Throne, USS Texas, Thulian Archives, the Artillery Bunker and Overlook.

08

Run a server. It’s one click now.

The nine servers in the browser exist because ordinary people put them up. The launcher is a small WPF app: toggle Multiplayer or Zombies, pick maps, gametypes, score limits and bot settings, name your server in colour with the swatch row (^1 red, ^3 yellow, ^7 white) and watch the preview — then hit Launch.

  1. Point it at the game

    The launcher finds the game folder on its own. A spare box with the game files copied over — no Steam installed — becomes a dedicated server.

  2. Configure in the GUI

    Maps, gametypes, score limits, bot fill, coloured server name. No config files to hand-edit, no command line.

  3. Hit Launch

    One click and the server is up, listed in the in-game browser for everyone else on this page.

And it starts every time

Roughly one launch in ten used to die a few seconds in. The cause was a repair guard in the game’s anti-tamper layer that quietly restored three of the client’s own patches — after which an integrity check failed and the process was shut down.

The guard is now filtered like the rest: forty cold starts in a row reached a full match with bots, against nine of ten before.
