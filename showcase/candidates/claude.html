01 — The only question that matters

“Isn’t it dead?”

Fair question. Retail matchmaking is empty and the progression servers are long gone. So here is the honest answer, with the receipts, before anything else on this page tries to sell you something.

Nine dedicated servers, run by four different people. Prop Hunt on three of them — a gametype nobody expects to see running on this engine. Host names come through in colour because the launcher’s colour codes work end to end.

That is the true state of it. The servers are real, they are joinable, and at the moment that screenshot was taken nobody was on any of them.

Which would be the end of the story, except for the thing that makes a small community playable at all: you are never actually alone in there.

02 — Why an empty server still gives you a game

Join at 3am. Get a full 9v9.

Set bot_fill 17 and every match fills to the slot count on map start — Team Deathmatch, Domination, Free-for-All, Hardpoint, Kill Confirmed, and War with complete nine-a-side teams. When a real player joins, a bot makes room for them.

This is the part most revival projects get wrong. Bots here are not a consolation prize for a dead game; they are the thing that lets a community of forty people keep nine servers worth playing on.

Three name pools, so the lobby isn’t the same lobby

54 names per pool, reshuffled every map. Default keeps the engine’s military codenames. Modern reads like 2016–2026. Nostalgia is the Xbox 360 lobby you remember.

03 — From here to a match

Four steps, about three minutes

  1. Own the game on Steam

    Call of Duty: WWII, the 2017 release. S2x ships no game content — it needs the files you already paid for.

  2. Download and extract

    Drop the contents of the release ZIP into the game folder, next to s2_mp64_ship.exe.

  3. Start Steam, then launch S2x

    Run s2x.exe. Steam has to be running; you don’t have to launch the game from it.

  4. Open the server browser and pick one

    Or start a Custom Match on your own and set bot_fill 17. Either way you are in a full game inside a minute.

04 — The part that was switched off

Your unlocks work again

Orders, contracts, payroll, the Quartermaster, Mail, supply drops — the whole Headquarters economy, re-implemented locally over the Achievement Engine protocol. It is not a cosmetic stand-in. The timers tick, the credits accrue, the drops land in your inventory.

And every soldier level-up now actually pays out the Rare Supply Drop the end-of-match screen has been promising you since 2017. The game always reported the level-up; there was simply nothing left on the other end to credit it.

05 — And then there is the other game

Nazi Zombies, with its own economy

Not a side note. Groesten Haus and The Final Reich play through with full progression — wall buys, the mystery box, consumables, wave tracking — and the Headquarters economy extends into it with a separate set of orders, contracts and skull-backed supply drops.

Kill zombies, survive waves, land headshots; the progress bars move while you play and pay out in Armory Credits and Rare Zombie Supply Drops.

06 — If you want to run one

A spare box and one click

The launcher is a Windows app with a mode toggle for Multiplayer and Zombies. Pick your maps, gametypes, score limits and bot settings, colour the server name with the swatch row, watch the preview, hit Launch. It finds the game folder on its own — a spare PC with the game files copied across and no Steam installed becomes a dedicated server.

The bug that made hosting miserable

Roughly one launch in ten used to die a few seconds in. The cause was a repair guard inside the game’s anti-tamper layer, quietly restoring three of the client’s own patches; an integrity check then failed and the process was shut down.

The guard is now filtered like the rest of that layer.
